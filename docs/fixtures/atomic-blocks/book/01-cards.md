# Atomic Blocks

Every card below carries one declaration: `break-inside: avoid`. Print honours
it, so a card that does not fit moves whole to the next page. The preview has
to agree, down to which page each heading lands on.

## Cards That Must Not Split

<div class="card">

A card is atomic because a reader uses it as a unit. Half a stat block on one page and half on the next is not a saving of paper, it is a card that has to be read twice, and the second reading is the one that gets it wrong at the table.

The cost of the rule is a short page. When a card will not fit the space that is left, the whole card moves down and the bottom of the previous page stays empty. That white band is the price of the guarantee, and it is a price book designers pay on purpose.

What the rule must never do is depend on where it is read. A card that stays whole in the PDF and splits in the preview teaches the author to fix a problem that is not in their book, and hides the short pages that are.

</div>

Nothing else on this page is unusual. Ordinary paragraphs flow and break wherever they land, which is what makes the cards' behaviour visible by contrast rather than by measurement.
