# Specimens

<div class="specimens">

Each stage below shows one specialty intro the way a book prints it, so the five can be compared on one page. In a book every intro opens on a left-hand page; here they sit one under another.

A dry line reads short and flatters the surveyor, and a flattered surveyor sets his stakes too close together and runs out of bank before he runs out of rope.

A stake that has moved is not always a stake that has failed. Silt travels, and a channel that ran hard against the eastern bank one winter will have shouldered its way west by autumn.

Oyster ground is surveyed by sound rather than by sight. A pole dropped on live bed rings; a pole dropped on dead bed thuds and keeps thudding.

Charts age badly here. A chart is a claim about where the water was on a particular afternoon, and the water has no obligation to honour it.

The ledger records the position, the date, and the tide; it does not attempt to record the reason.
