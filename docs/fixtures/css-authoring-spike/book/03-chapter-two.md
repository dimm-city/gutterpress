# A Foraging Gallery

The tidepool at the mouth of the channel is the richest stop on the
whole route — a shallow basin that stays flooded even at the lowest
tides, sheltering anemones, hermit crabs, and the occasional stranded
sculpin. The plate on the next page was shot an hour before a spring
low, when the pool was at its clearest.

## Bringing It Home

Whatever comes home from the marsh should be cleaned the same day.
Bivalves purge best in a bucket of clean saltwater for an hour before
cooking; crabs keep only a day or two on ice. None of this is
complicated, but skipping it is the single most common reason a good
morning's foraging ends up in the trash instead of on the table.

Next season's almanac will cover the spring shellfish closures in
detail — for now, this edition ends where the tide table always does:
with a reminder to check tomorrow's numbers before you plan tomorrow's
trip.
